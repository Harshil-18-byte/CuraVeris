This notice is served under the following legal provisions and I demand that your administration act upon it immediately:

Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — Section 2(47) read with Section 86: Withholding a patient who has been declared medically fit for discharge as a means of coercing payment of a disputed bill constitutes an unfair trade practice and deficiency of service under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. This Act provides for complaints to the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission and allows for compensation including punitive damages.
Indian Penal Code — Section 340 (Wrongful Confinement): Preventing a person who is legally entitled to leave from doing so may constitute wrongful confinement under Section 340 of the Indian Penal Code, which is punishable under Section 342.
Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010: Under the Clinical Establishments Act, hospitals are required to provide emergency treatment and cannot deny services or discharge on grounds of non-payment in cases where the treatment has already been provided.
Supreme Court of India — Guidelines on Patient Rights: The Supreme Court of India and various High Courts have repeatedly held that hospitals cannot hold patients hostage over billing disputes and that such conduct violates fundamental rights under Article 21 of the Constitution of India (Right to Life and Personal Liberty).
